HARDWARE-BACKED SSH KEYS, CERTIFIED BY A PASSKEY.

Protect your SSH keys from theft.

Not at the cost of convenience.

Keyringd is an SSH agent that keeps your private keys in your computer’s hardware.

Hardware-protected keys can’t be extracted by an attacker, but they’re bound to a single device. With Keyringd, that doesn’t mean starting over when you replace your computer.

Instead of transferring the private key, Keyringd uses your passkey as an SSH certificate authority (CA) to certify a new device key for your next computer. Once your servers trust that CA, their setup stays the same across computers.

For everyday SSH, your device key does the work—without asking for your passkey each time.

BEYOND PRIVATE KEY FILES

Use the key.
Keep the secret in hardware.

SSH private keys are typically stored as files. If your computer is compromised, those files can be stolen and used elsewhere.

Keyringd keeps each computer's private key in its TPM or Secure Enclave. The hardware signs your connections without letting the private key leave.

DEVICE CHANGES, SIMPLIFIED BY SSH CERTIFICATES

New computer.
Same server setup.

A hardware-backed private key cannot move to your next computer. Normally, replacing the computer means creating a new key and registering it on every server that trusted the old one.

With Keyringd, your servers trust your passkey’s CA public key instead. Your passkey certifies each computer’s device key, so replacing your computer requires no changes to your server configuration.

Use your passkey when setting up a computer. Everyday SSH connections use its device key—no passkey authentication, no security key to carry.

SERVER TRUST

Two ways to connect.

Choose the setup supported by your SSH server or service.

SSH CERTIFICATES

For servers that support SSH certificates

Register your passkey’s CA public key once. Connect with certified device keys, and keep the same server registration when you add or replace a computer.

Set up certificate authentication →
PUBLIC KEYS

For servers that do not support SSH certificates

Register both your device key’s public key and your passkey’s plain public key for direct authentication. Use the device key for everyday connections and your passkey after replacing your computer, until you register the new device key.
